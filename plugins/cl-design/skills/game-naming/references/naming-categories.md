# Naming categories, anti-patterns, and rubric

## Four structural categories

| Category | How it is built | Availability | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Invented word | Morph roots, fuse syllables, borrow from Latin, Greek, or a craft vocabulary | Highest | Sounds like a pharmaceutical; hard to spell |
| Compound or blend | Two real words fused, the fusion carrying the promise | High if the pair is unusual | Both words generic; too long |
| Repurposed real word | A real word lifted from its normal context, often with a prefix or suffix for availability | Medium | Dictionary word alone is contested; prefixes like get or play fix it |
| Altered spelling | Drop or swap letters while the word stays recognizable | Medium | Dated fast; needs spelling out on calls |

Game-development vocabulary is a rich source for repurposed words: loop, tick, frame, crunchless, spawn, respawn, checkpoint, loadout, greybox, blockout, juice, hitstop, coyote time, vertical slice, ship, crew, forge, wright, smith. Nautical and crew vocabulary works for anything about shipping: deckhand, bosun, keel, helm, berth, shipwright. Many of these are already brands; treat them as prompts, not answers.

## Anti-patterns (kill before scoring)

- Generic category word alone (game, studio, kit, labs, tools) or a stack of them.
- "X for Y" or "the Z of games"; reads as a pitch, not a name.
- Trendy suffixes that signal a clone of a famous product.
- Four or more syllables, or any name that needs spelling out on a call.
- Accidental meanings in the audience's languages (check Spanish, French, Portuguese, German, Japanese, Mandarin, Tagalog, Hindi as a minimum when the audience is global).
- A famous mark in the same field one letter away.
- Hard consonant clusters at the start for non-English speakers; silent letters.

## Rubric (score 1 to 5, weight to the brief)

- Distinct: no confusion with a known product in games or developer tools.
- Sayable: read once, said right by most people.
- Spellable: heard once, typed right.
- Meaning: carries the promise or an insider wink the audience gets without explanation.
- Fit: matches the three adjectives of the vibe.
- Room: still fits when the product line grows (a studio name, a kit, a tool).

## Domain strategy

- Exact `.com` is rare for short names; a modifier (`get`, `play`, `try`, `hq`, `kit`) or an ending that fits the field (`.dev`, `.gg`, `.games`, `.studio`) is normal and does not hurt a developer-facing brand.
- Keep the name itself clean; put the modifier only in the domain.
- Check that the handle you want exists on the two or three networks the audience uses; consistency beats perfection.
